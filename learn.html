How to start with nothing.

No course, no upsell, no link in bio to a $5,000 program. This is what I actually do, written down.

One — houses

You don't need money. You need to find the person who has a problem that isn't price.

Every discount comes from a constraint. Nobody sells below market because you negotiated well — they sell because something else in their life costs more than the money does. Your whole job is finding those people before anyone else.

  • Grass three feet high — nobody's been there in months
  • Mail piled at the door — owner isn't local
  • Boarded window, no permit posted — they've stopped spending on it
  • "My aunt passed and left us a place" — heirs almost never want the house
  • A landlord who just did an eviction — that's the moment they're done

Write down the address. Look up the owner on your county auditor's site — it's free and public. Send a letter or knock. Most people never do this once.

You do not need a license to bring me an address. You do need one to represent someone in a sale — different thing. If you find one I buy, I pay you a flat finder's fee.
Two — businesses

Buying a business with none of your own money is more normal than buying a house that way.

Roughly 10,000 people turn 65 every day in this country and a lot of them own a business with no one to leave it to. They care more about it surviving than about getting every dollar at closing. That's what makes seller financing normal here and rare in housing.

The two structures that need almost nothing from you:

  • Seller financing — the owner takes payments out of the profits the business already makes. You're buying it with its own money.
  • SBA 7(a) — a government-backed loan built for this. Often around 10% down, and a seller note can sometimes cover part of that.

The one number that matters is SDE — seller's discretionary earnings. What the business actually puts in the owner's pocket: profit, plus their salary, plus personal expenses run through it.

Asking price ÷ SDE = the multiple

Under 2×   — cheap. Ask why. Usually a reason.
2× to 3.5× — normal for a small business
Over 4×    — you're paying for someone's optimism

A business earning $200,000 SDE at a 3× multiple is $600,000. If it services its own debt out of that $200,000 and still pays you, the price works. If it only works when you assume growth, it doesn't work.

Ask for three years of filed tax returns, not a spreadsheet. A seller who won't show returns is telling you the answer.
Three — the honest part

What nobody selling you a course will say.

Finding it is the hard part. Not the money — there's always money for a genuinely good deal. Not the math — it's subtraction. The hard part is being the person who noticed first, and that takes months of looking at things other people scroll past.

Most people quit before they've looked at fifty. The ones who make it are boring about it — they drive the same streets, read the same filings, call the same brokers, and one day something breaks their way.

You will not need $5,000 to learn this. You'll need about ninety days of paying attention.
