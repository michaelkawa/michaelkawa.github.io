Every asset has two prices. I live in the gap.

Houses, watches, whatever has a real market and a lazy price. Buy under, exit at value, keep the difference. That's the entire business.

Nobody pays you for effort. They pay you for the distance between what a thing cost you and what it's actually worth. Everything else is logistics.

Lesson one

The discount isn't negotiation. It's a constraint.

A gap doesn't appear because you talked someone down. It appears because the seller has a problem that isn't price. They're out of state. They inherited it. It's a headache they've been avoiding for two years. They need money this month, not in ninety days.

You aren't taking advantage of them — you're the only person offering to make it disappear tomorrow. That convenience is what you get paid for, and it's why the gap exists at all.

Lesson two

Same rule, different asset.

A watch owner who needs liquidity this week sells under market for exactly the same reason a landlord who's done being a landlord does. The category changes. The constraint doesn't. Once you see it, you see it everywhere — cars, equipment, inventory, houses, steel on a bracelet.

Lesson three

Finding one is the hard part.

Not the money — there's always money for a real deal. Not the skill — the math is subtraction. The whole job is finding a constrained seller before anyone else does. That's why most people never do this: they wait for deals to be listed, and by the time something's listed the gap is gone.

Which is the good news, because constraints are visible from the outside:

  • Grass three feet high — nobody's been there in months
  • Mail piled at the door — owner isn't local
  • Boarded window, no permit — they've stopped spending on it
  • "My aunt passed and left us a place" — heirs almost never want the house
  • A dealer selling under market to make payroll — same thing, smaller box

You don't need a course or capital to notice any of that. You need to be paying attention on a street somebody else isn't watching.
